Dayhome vs Daycare in Calgary: What's the Difference, and Which One Is Right for Your Family?

By Zakiyah · Updated June 10, 2026 · 4 min read

If you have ever fallen down the late-night rabbit hole of searching "dayhome vs daycare Calgary" with a baby asleep on your chest, you are not alone. The two words get tossed around like they mean the same thing. They do not. And knowing the difference between a dayhome and a daycare can save you a lot of second-guessing when you are choosing care for the most important little person in your life.

So let's break it down in plain language.

What is a daycare?

A daycare, or child care centre, is a larger, facility-based program. Think of a dedicated building, multiple rooms split by age group, and a team of staff. Centres can care for dozens of children at once. They tend to run on a fixed schedule, have set drop-off windows, and feel a bit more like a small school. For some families that structure is exactly what they want.

What is a dayhome?

A dayhome, also called a family day home, is child care offered in someone's actual home. It is smaller, cosier, and more personal. In Alberta, a family day home educator can care for a maximum of six children at a time, not including their own kids. That small group is the whole point. Your child is not one of thirty. They are one of a handful, with the same caring adult every single day.

For a lot of NW Calgary parents, that consistency is the deciding factor. Babies and toddlers thrive on routine and a familiar face. A dayhome gives them that in a way a big rotating staff roster sometimes cannot.

The big differences at a glance

Size and ratio. A dayhome keeps numbers tiny, with a hard cap of six children. A centre cares for far more children across the whole building.

Setting. A dayhome looks and feels like a home, because it is one. Home-cooked meals, a backyard, nap time in a quiet room, a real couch for story time. A centre is purpose-built and more institutional.

Flexibility. Dayhomes often offer more give on hours, part-time spots, and the kind of "my meeting ran late, I am ten minutes behind" grace that busy parents quietly pray for.

Cost. Here is the part that surprises people. Thanks to Alberta's Affordability Grant, approved dayhomes and licensed daycares charge the same flat parent fee. We wrote a whole post on exactly what a dayhome costs in Calgary, so head there if you want the numbers.

"Approved" vs "licensed" matters too

One thing that trips parents up: daycares are licensed, while individual family day homes are approved through a licensed agency. It sounds like a technicality, but it tells you a lot about the safety checks and oversight behind the care. We unpack what an approved dayhome actually means, because it is worth understanding before you enrol anywhere.

So which one should you choose?

There is no universally right answer, only the right answer for your family. Ask yourself a few honest questions.

Does your child do better in a busy, stimulating environment, or do they melt down in noise and crowds? Do you want a school-like structure, or a homey rhythm? Is flexibility on pickup time a make-or-break for your work life? Do you want your kid to have the same trusted adult every day, or are you comfortable with a rotating team?

If your gut leans toward small, warm, consistent, and personal, a dayhome is probably your fit. If you want scale, structure, and a building full of resources, a centre may suit you better.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a dayhome and a daycare in Calgary?

A daycare is a larger facility-based centre with many children and a staff team. A dayhome is small, home-based care for a maximum of six children with one consistent educator.

Is a dayhome cheaper than a daycare in Calgary?

No. Under Alberta's Affordability Grant, approved dayhomes and licensed daycares charge the same flat monthly parent fee, so you choose on fit, not price.

Are dayhomes regulated in Alberta?

Licensed family dayhomes operate under a government-licensed agency with safety checks and regular monitoring.
